A zipper is easiest to read when it's halfway closed. On one side of the slider, two rows of teeth face each other across a gap. On the other, the teeth form one chain. The interesting part is the short stretch you can't see, under the bit of metal you're pulling.

## Follow one tooth

If you could lift the lid off the slider, you'd find a [Y-shaped channel](https://www.ykk.com/english/ykk/tech/01.html). The wide end has two entrances, one for each row. The narrow end has room for the joined chain. When you close the zipper, the slider moves toward the open rows. From the point of view of a tooth, the trip goes the other way. It enters through one arm of the Y and leaves through the stem.

The two rows are staggered, so a tooth coming in on the left meets a gap between teeth on the right. Look at the enlarged tooth in figure 4 of [Gideon Sundback's 1917 patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1219881A/en). One face has a rounded bump and the other has a matching hollow. As the rows come together, each bump nests in the hollow of a neighboring tooth. The teeth can rock a little without coming apart, which is why the closed fastener in the patent can bend instead of behaving like a rigid strip of metal.

Opening the zipper sends the joined chain into the narrow end first. Inside the slider, the divider sends each row back into its own arm of the Y. Each tooth gets pried loose in turn. There's no need to pull the whole seam apart at once.

## What holds after the slider leaves?

The teeth behind the slider stay joined on their own. Keeping the *slider* from creeping back down is a separate job. [YKK shows a jeans zipper](https://www.ykk.com/english/ykk/tech/03.html) with a small pin that catches on the teeth when the pull tab lies flat. Lift the tab and the pin lifts out of the way. That explains something I'd otherwise put down to a stiff zipper: on that design, the angle of the tab matters before the slider has moved at all.

The next time you zip a coat, stop in the middle and look at the first joined teeth just past the slider. Your fingers are on the pull tab, but the coat closes in those few millimeters at the mouth of the slider. Move the tab a little farther. The fork moves with it.
